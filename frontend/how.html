How it works

How we plan a walk worth taking, and what's behind it.

Your walk

  • You say how long you have and what you feel like, typed or spoken: "25 minutes, quiet streets, coffee at the end". You check what was understood before anything is planned.
  • From your start, one search on our walking map finds every place you can reach in time: cafes, bakeries, bookstores, parks, ice cream, landmarks. Places known to be closed when you'd arrive are left out.
  • Each option is scored on how well it fits your wish and your minutes; you see up to six, best first, and can swipe through them. Then you start walking with turn-by-turn directions on this page, and short intros to places you pass.
  • Your start point is used for this plan only. It is not stored.

With a friend: a fair meeting point

  • Walking times come from our own walking map of Manhattan, northern Brooklyn, Long Island City, and Jersey City, Hoboken and their neighbors across the Hudson, built from OpenStreetMap, at 80 m per minute: a little slower than routing apps, to leave time for crossings. Against openrouteservice, the median difference is 0.83 min. No footpath crosses the Hudson below the George Washington Bridge, so a start in New Jersey and one in Manhattan are usually too far apart for a walk together.
  • From each start, one search finds how long it takes to reach every street corner. Corners where you two arrive within 2 minutes of each other, inside the first 60% of the walk, become meeting points, along with named parks, plazas and gates near the middle.
  • From there you walk together to a place that fits what you asked for (a cafe, a park, a bookstore...), within your time budget: a few minutes of slack, or none if you set a hard limit. Places known to be closed are left out. You see up to six options.
  • If nothing fits, StrollCall says why and suggests what would work, such as "needs about 40 minutes".

What the Meta models do

Muse SparkOn your own: reads your wish ("25 minutes, quiet, coffee at the end") and which place features it asks for; those decide the order of your options, which our planner scores on our data. It also wrote the place intros ahead of time (below). With a friend, it adds two-person work: it balances both people's wishes, ranks the options of the fair meetup plan with a short reason for each, and writes the invite line. Every answer is checked: it must fit the schema, and every number and place feature must come from our data. If it doesn't, rule-based text is used instead, and the page says so.
Muse ImageThe 24 illustrations, made ahead of time and picked by the kind of walk.
Muse Voice TranscribeThe Speak button: your words become text you can edit before it's read.
Muse GlimmerMeta's open-weights model (meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B, full BF16 weights), on our own server at Modal (one H200 GPU). When it is running, it reads your wish instead of Muse Spark, with the same instructions and the same checks; the page then says "Read by Muse Glimmer (open weights, on Modal)". It starts only for set windows (a GPU costs by the hour), and if it doesn't answer within 3 seconds, Muse Spark reads the wish and the page says so. Every other job stays on the Meta API.
Muse Spark OpenBuilt as a backup for the text jobs (Meta's open-weight model on a hosted provider). It is switched off and not used today.

When Muse Glimmer reads your wish, the words go to our own server at Modal and not to Meta's API. What Meta receives: the text you type or speak, place names and features, walking minutes and option labels. Never your start point or any coordinates; a test checks every request for them. StrollCall runs on Meta's free credits, so Meta may use what you type or say to improve its products. Please don't include personal details.

Place intros on walks

Parks, gardens, notable buildings, artworks, memorials, viewpoints, fountains, bridges and landmarks you pass can show a short intro. Each one starts from the place's English Wikipedia summary. Muse Spark shortened it to at most two sentences using only that text, and a check dropped any intro that added a number, a name, a feature or praise not in the source: 525 kept, 57 dropped. Every card links its Wikipedia article (CC BY-SA 4.0). The intros were written ahead of time; no model runs while you walk.

When Meta is unavailable

The route and the meeting point never depend on Meta. If a Meta model can't answer, StrollCall uses rule-based text and marks that part "rule-based" or "Demo data". It never presents it as model output.

Privacy, briefly

Your start point is used to plan your walk and is not stored. With a friend, it is never shown to them and is deleted when the invite expires after 24 hours. Your live position stays on your phone. All the details.

How well it works

  • With a friend, 12 test pairs across the map: the right outcome in 12 of 12 (a plan where one exists, an honest "too far" where it doesn't). Median arrival gap 0.2 min, median time-budget error 0.4 min. Report
  • With a friend, 10 wish cases written down before testing: the top pick met 7 of 10 wishes with Muse Spark, 4 of 10 with rules only. Report
  • Planning on the server takes well under a second; the Muse Spark steps take most of the time. With a friend and Meta answering live, the plan shows about 3-4 s after your friend accepts, and Muse Spark's explanation follows a few seconds later (median of 3 runs). Report
  • Place intros: report. All code and reports: github.com/zh315-bit/strollcall.

Small tests by the team, not a study. A user test is planned for Nov 2-3.
