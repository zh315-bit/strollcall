How it works

A fair place to meet, a short walk together, and what's behind it.

A fair meeting point

  • Walking times come from our own walking map of Manhattan, northern Brooklyn, Long Island City, and Jersey City, Hoboken and their neighbors across the Hudson, built from OpenStreetMap, at 80 m per minute: a little slower than routing apps, to leave time for crossings. Against openrouteservice, the median difference is 0.83 min. No footpath crosses the Hudson below the George Washington Bridge, so a start in New Jersey and one in Manhattan are usually too far apart for a walk together.
  • From each start, one search finds how long it takes to reach every street corner. Corners where you two arrive within 2 minutes of each other, inside the first 60% of the walk, become meeting points, along with named parks, plazas and gates near the middle.
  • From there you walk together to a place that fits what you asked for (a cafe, a park, a bookstore...), within your time budget: a few minutes of slack, or none if you set a hard limit. Places known to be closed are left out. You see the best three.
  • If nothing fits, StrollCall says why and suggests what would work, such as "needs about 40 minutes".

What the Meta models do

Muse SparkReads your wish ("25 minutes, quiet, coffee at the end"), balances both people's wishes, ranks the six options with reasons, and writes the invite line. Every answer is checked: it must fit the schema, and every number and place feature must come from our data. If it doesn't, rule-based text is used instead, and the page says so.
Muse ImageThe 24 invite illustrations, made ahead of time and picked by the kind of walk.
Muse Voice TranscribeThe Speak button: your words become text you can edit before it's read.
Muse Spark OpenBuilt as a backup for the text jobs (Meta's open-weight model on a hosted provider). It is switched off and not used today.

Place intros on walks

Parks, artworks, memorials and landmarks you pass can show a short intro. Each one starts from the place's English Wikipedia summary. Muse Spark shortened it to at most two sentences using only that text, and a check dropped any intro that added a number, a name, a feature or praise not in the source: 525 kept, 57 dropped. Every card links its Wikipedia article (CC BY-SA 4.0). The intros were written ahead of time; no model runs while you walk.

When Meta is unavailable

The route and the meeting point never depend on Meta. If a Meta model can't answer, StrollCall uses rule-based text and marks that part "rule-based" or "Demo data". It never presents it as model output.

Privacy, briefly

Start points are used to plan, never shown to the other person, and deleted when the invite expires after 24 hours. Your live position stays on your phone. All the details.

How well it works

  • 12 test pairs across the map: the right outcome in 12 of 12 (a plan where one exists, an honest "too far" where it doesn't). Median arrival gap 0.2 min, median time-budget error 0.4 min. Report
  • 10 wish cases written down before testing: the top pick met 7 of 10 wishes with Muse Spark, 4 of 10 with rules only. Report
  • Planning on the server takes well under a second; each Muse Spark step adds about 1-2 s. Report
  • Place intros: report. All code and reports: github.com/zh315-bit/strollcall.

Small tests by the team, not a study. A user test with friends is planned for Nov 2-3.
